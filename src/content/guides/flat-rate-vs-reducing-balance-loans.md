Two lenders can both say "5% interest" and charge you very different amounts. The difference is usually whether the rate is **flat** or on a **reducing balance**, and whether it is charged **per month** or **per year**. This guide explains the difference with real numbers. You can try your own in the [Loan Calculator](/loan-calculator).

## The short answer

- **Reducing balance:** interest is worked out on the amount you still owe. As you repay, the interest gets smaller.
- **Flat rate:** interest is worked out once on the full amount you borrowed, for the whole loan, and added on top. You pay interest on money you have already paid back.

For the same quoted rate, a flat loan always costs more than a reducing-balance loan.

## Example 1: ₦500,000 for 6 months at 5% a month

| | Flat | Reducing balance |
|---|---|---|
| You pay each month | ₦108,333.33 | ₦98,508.73 |
| Total interest | ₦150,000.00 | ₦91,052.40 |
| Total you repay | ₦650,000.00 | ₦591,052.40 |
| Real yearly cost | about 96.6% | 60.0% |

The same "5% a month" costs you **₦58,947.60 more** when it is flat.

## Example 2: ₦1,000,000 for 12 months at 20% a year

| | Flat | Reducing balance |
|---|---|---|
| You pay each month | ₦100,000.00 | ₦92,634.51 |
| Total interest | ₦200,000.00 | ₦111,614.07 |
| Total you repay | ₦1,200,000.00 | ₦1,111,614.07 |
| Real yearly cost | about 35.1% | 20.0% |

A flat "20% a year" behaves like a reducing-balance rate of about 35% a year. Over this loan, that is **₦88,385.93 more**.

## What is the "real yearly cost"?

It is one number that lets you compare any two loans fairly: the reducing-balance interest rate per year that would give the same monthly payments. A flat 20% a year looks like 20%, but because you are paying the loan back all the time, you do not really have the full amount for the full year. The real yearly cost shows what you effectively pay for the money you actually have.

The [Loan Calculator](/loan-calculator) works this out for you, and it also works for loans quoted per month.

## Monthly rates are bigger than they look

A rate of 5% a month sounds small, but it is 60% a year if you simply add up twelve months, and more once interest builds on interest. Always check whether a rate is per month or per year before you compare.

## Questions to ask before you borrow

1. **Is the rate per month or per year?**
2. **Is it a flat rate or on a reducing balance?**
3. **What is the total amount I will repay**, in naira, by the last payment?
4. **Are there other charges**, such as processing fees, insurance, or tax on the interest? These make the loan cost more than the interest alone.
5. **What happens if I pay early or late?** Some loans charge for early repayment, and most charge for late payment.
6. **Can I see everything in writing** before I sign?

The most useful number is usually the **total you repay**. Compare that across lenders, not just the percentage.

## Things the calculator does not include

The calculator covers interest only. It assumes equal monthly payments, made at the end of each month. Lenders may count days instead of months, round differently, or add fees, so their figure can differ a little. If the difference is large, ask the lender to explain it.
