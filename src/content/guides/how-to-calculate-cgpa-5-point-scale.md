Your GPA is one number that sums up how well you did, and your CGPA is the same idea for all your semesters together. This guide shows how both are worked out, step by step, on the 5.0 scale used by most Nigerian universities. You can follow it with a pen, or let the [CGPA Calculator](/cgpa-calculator) do the sum.

## The short answer

For each course, multiply its grade points by its credit units. Add all those results, then divide by the total credit units. That is your GPA.

## Step 1: grade points

On the 5.0 scale, each letter grade is worth a number of points:

| Grade | A | B | C | D | E | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Points | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

## Step 2: a worked example

Suppose you took four courses this semester:

| Course | Credit units | Grade | Points | Quality points (units × points) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MTH 101 | 3 | A | 5 | 15 |
| PHY 102 | 2 | B | 4 | 8 |
| CHM 103 | 4 | C | 3 | 12 |
| GST 104 | 1 | F | 0 | 0 |
| **Total** | **10** | | | **35** |

Your semester GPA is the total quality points divided by the total credit units: 35 ÷ 10 = **3.50**.

Notice that the 4-unit course counted four times as much as the 1-unit course. That is why a big course matters more than a small one.

## Step 3: working out your new CGPA

Your CGPA combines this semester with everything before it. Imagine you already had a CGPA of 3.80 after 60 credit units:

1. Quality points you already have: 3.80 × 60 = 228.
2. Add this semester's quality points: 228 + 35 = 263.
3. Add the units: 60 + 10 = 70.
4. Divide: 263 ÷ 70 = 3.757, which rounds to **3.76**.

Your CGPA went down a little, from 3.80 to 3.76, because this semester's GPA of 3.50 was lower than your earlier average.

## The common degree classes

Most Nigerian universities use bands like these on the 5.0 scale:

| CGPA | Class |
|---|---|
| 4.50 to 5.00 | First Class |
| 3.50 to 4.49 | Second Class Upper (2:1) |
| 2.40 to 3.49 | Second Class Lower (2:2) |
| 1.50 to 2.39 | Third Class |
| 1.00 to 1.49 | Pass |

These are the bands usually published for Nigerian universities. They are not an official ruling for your school, so check your school's handbook or ask your department. Some private universities use a 4.0 scale instead, and this calculator does not cover those.

## What GPA do I need next semester?

You can work backwards. Say you have a CGPA of 3.80 after 60 units, and you will take 20 more units. To finish with a CGPA of 3.90, you need 3.90 × 80 units = 312 quality points in total. You already have 228, so you need 84 more from the 20 units. That is 84 ÷ 20 = a semester GPA of **4.20**.

The same method works for any target. Multiply the target by the total units you will have, subtract the points you already have, and divide by the units you are about to take. If the answer is above 5.00, the target cannot be reached in one semester.

## Things that can make your result differ

- **Rounding.** Schools round in different ways. The calculator rounds to two decimal places.
- **Repeated or carried-over courses.** Some schools replace the old grade and some count both attempts. Ask your department how yours works.
- **Courses that carry no credit.** Some courses are pass or fail and do not count towards your CGPA.

If the calculator and your result sheet disagree by more than a hundredth or two, check the credit units first, because a wrong unit count is the most common cause.
